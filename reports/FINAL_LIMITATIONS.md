# Final Limitations Register

> Every listed limitation states its analytical consequence, not just the fact of the limitation. These are not excuses for the results obtained -- they are the boundary conditions within which every result in this project should be read.

## Measurement / physical interpretation

- **B-factors are proxies for crystallographic mobility/disorder, not direct solution dynamics.** A crystallographic B-factor reflects a mixture of true atomic thermal motion, static disorder (multiple conformers averaged in the crystal), and model/refinement error. **Consequence**: every "flexibility" claim in this project is a claim about relative crystallographic mobility within a structure, never a direct measurement of solution-phase dynamics (e.g. from NMR or MD); the word "flexibility" is used throughout as shorthand for this proxy, and figures/summaries say so explicitly where the claim could otherwise be over-read.
- **Resolution, refinement protocol, and crystal-packing confounding.** B-factor scale and distribution shift with resolution and refinement software/protocol; crystal contacts can artificially rigidify surface loops. **Consequence**: per-instance Z-normalization (this project's core design decision) removes each *instance's own* scale, but cannot remove a *systematic* difference between two groups of structures solved at different average resolution or packing environment. The apo/holo QC-balance audit (`reports/tables/apo_holo_qc_balance.csv`) found 3/9 eligible receptors with a resolution/R-free/coverage imbalance between their APO and HOLO groups; one of those (PPARgamma) was specifically re-tested in a resolution-overlap-restricted band and the effect survived, but this check was not performed exhaustively for every nominal result project-wide.

## Sampling / coverage

- **Uneven PDB sampling among receptors.** PDB entry counts per receptor range from 0 to 379 (PPARgamma) with a median around 10-19; a handful of well-studied drug-target receptors dominate the raw structural record. **Consequence**: every cross-receptor comparison in this project uses the receptor (not the PDB entry) as the unit of inference specifically to prevent this imbalance from dominating superfamily-level conclusions (demonstrated directly in `reports/tables/entry_level_overrepresentation.csv`: the top 5 receptors are 60% of all entries); any figure or table that does report entry-level detail is explicitly labeled secondary/descriptive.
- **Incomplete structural-segment mapping.** Even after the full v3 curation effort, several segments remain unresolved: H8 has 0% support anywhere in the 42-receptor coordinate cohort (never fabricated to fill the gap); H2, H4, H6, H9, H10 fall below the 50% feature-selection threshold used for the primary clustering matrix. **Consequence**: the primary PCA/clustering feature set is 8 segments, not the full canonical 12+beta-sheet vocabulary; any conclusion about "the LBD" is really a conclusion about the subset of segments with adequate structural evidence, stated as such throughout.
- **Only 29/42 coordinate receptors met the clustering completeness criterion** (>=60% of the 8 primary segments observed). 13 receptors, including the anchor receptor VDR itself (whose own template lacks confident H5/H7/H11), were excluded from the primary clustering cohort -- an analysis-specific exclusion, never a removal from `structures.csv` or any master file. **Consequence**: the "no robust discrete clusters" conclusion applies to this 29-receptor cohort's flexibility profile, not literally every project receptor.
- **Apo/holo imbalance.** Only 9/42 coordinate receptors (7 at the pre-declared PRIMARY >=5/5 tier) have enough APO and HOLO PDB coverage to test at all; 33 receptors, including VDR (0 APO, 48 HOLO PDB entries), cannot be assessed for an apo/holo effect with current PDB coverage. **Consequence**: the apo/holo results describe a small, non-random subset of well-studied receptors; they cannot be extrapolated to receptors with no APO structures at all.
- **Receptor grouping sample sizes are small.** The largest usable functional group (Nutritional sensors, n=25) still yields only moderate statistical power for a Kruskal-Wallis test with 3 groups; the smallest (Orphan receptors, n=5) is underpowered for anything but a large effect. The formal NR subfamily-letter grouping (the most biologically precise scheme) could not be used as a formal test grouping at all -- every subfamily has n<=4. **Consequence**: a true small-to-moderate subfamily-level flexibility difference could exist and simply be undetectable (Type II error) with current PDB coverage; "no FDR-significant segment" is not proof of "no difference exists."

## Causal inference

- **Observational PDB comparisons do not prove ligand causality.** Every apo/holo and site-vs-background comparison in this project is an observational comparison across independently deposited structures, never a controlled experiment on one crystal. **Consequence**: language throughout uses "associated with" / "consistent with", never "causes" or "confirms mechanism" (explicitly enforced and tested in the specificity-validation stage's summary).
- **Mutations, construct boundaries, cofactor presence, and crystallization differences are uncontrolled confounders.** A HOLO structure may also differ from an APO structure of the same receptor in point mutations, construct length, the presence of a bound coactivator/corepressor peptide, or crystallization conditions -- none of which are adjusted for. **Consequence**: any single apo/holo or site-specific finding could in principle reflect one of these confounders rather than ligand state itself; the redundancy audit (`reports/tables/apo_holo_redundancy_audit.md`) checked for single-ligand dominance (found none) but publication/deposition-series and mutation metadata are not tracked in this pipeline at all, so that specific check could not be performed.
- **Multiple deposited PDBs may derive from related structural series** (the same lab, construct, or a set of close analogs solved together), which are not independent biological replicates even though they are independent depositions. **Consequence**: leave-one-PDB-out robustness checks were run for every significant result specifically to catch a result driven by one deposition or a tight cluster of related ones; results that failed this check are flagged explicitly (e.g. one nominal apo/holo result, `loop_H1_H3` in the PRIMARY subfamily grouping, retains p<0.05 in only 47% of leave-one-out folds and is treated as fragile, not evidence).

## Functional-site definition quality

- **Segment-level-proxy functional-site definitions for most receptors.** Outside the 5 curated anchor receptors, the coactivator groove and dimerization interface are usually defined as "the H3/H4/H5 (or H9/H10/H11) segment" rather than specific residues -- because tier B evidence (bound-coactivator-peptide or receptor-receptor contact geometry) was never computed, this pipeline does not track polymer-partner chain identity outside the historical strict-QC subset. **Consequence**: any site-vs-background result for these two site classes at the segment-proxy tier should be read as "flexibility of the region containing the site," not "flexibility of the site residues specifically" -- exactly the distinction the specificity-validation stage was built to test, and which resolved the coactivator groove's original apparent effect as STRUCTURAL_CONTEXT_EXPLAINED rather than site-specific.
- **Dimerization-interface residue-level evidence is limited to 2 anchor receptors** (ESR1, GR) with curated residues; the confirmatory matched-background superfamily reanalysis could not be run for this site class at all (INSUFFICIENT_RESIDUE_LEVEL_MAPPING). **Consequence**: no claim, positive or negative, is made about whether the dimerization interface is specifically more or less flexible than its structural context -- the evidence simply does not exist yet to decide either way.

## Statistical

- **Small n for confirmatory (matched-background) superfamily tests** (20 receptors for AF2/H12, 29 for the ligand-binding pocket, only 4 for the coactivator groove, 2 for the dimerization interface). **Consequence**: the two confirmed ("SITE_SPECIFIC_SUPPORTED") site classes have adequate power; the other two do not, and their lack of significance should not be read as evidence of absence.
- **BH-FDR family-size sensitivity.** As formally documented in the specificity-validation stage's FDR audit, a small within-receptor test family can produce a *harsher* correction than the same p-value's position in the full global pool -- 3 functional-site tests were global-significant but not within-receptor-significant purely from this effect, not from a bug. **Consequence**: within-receptor FDR remains the pre-declared PRIMARY significance criterion throughout; the global FDR is always reported alongside as context, never substituted.
